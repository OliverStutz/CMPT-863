## The course rubric for this section

> Related work (3 points). 1: Major related work is missing. 2: Much of the relevant related work is present, but either some is missing or its relationship to this project is weak. 3: Related work is present and well-represented. Its relationship to the project is clear.

The assignment brief says: "Discuss 4–5 relevant published papers, how they inform your project, and how your work extends them."

## What to judge

### A. Accuracy, claim by claim
For each of the five paragraphs (Strickland; Prike; Chen; Greitemeyer with Ecker & Rodricks and Mickelberg; Reif) and for the Aamir sentences in the Research question: list every factual claim, and mark each `Accurate`, `Inaccurate` or `Imprecise`, with the location in the paper. Give corrected wording for anything not accurate. Numbers, who did what, who was measured on what, what the checked person could do, whether the check was correct.

### B. Link to the hypotheses
The hypotheses are H1 (flag lowers trust), H2 (a reasoned answer restores trust, possibly above the neutral group), H3 (a denial lowers it further), H4 (the checker makes the board member question more, and the same answer then moves trust further in either direction). For each paragraph: does the stated "This informs H…" fit? Is the paper's relevance to that hypothesis stated in a way a marker can follow? Does the paragraph say how our study extends the paper? Note in particular whether H4 is served by any paragraph, since H4 was rewritten after the section.

### C. Coverage
Is major related work missing? Judge against the shortlist and your own knowledge of the literature (label knowledge from outside the files as such). Are any of the cited papers weak enough that a listed alternative (for example Arkes et al. 2007, von Schenk et al. 2024, Chae & Tewksbury 2026) would serve better? Are the two passing citations (Ecker & Rodricks; Mickelberg et al.) earning their place?

### D. Score and fixes
Give the rubric score you would award (1, 2 or 3) and the reason in two sentences. Then list the fixes, in order of importance, each as: what to change, the replacement wording, and why. Keep the wording plain: name what was measured and on whom, no statistics terms as a stand-in for a finding.

Write the report in Markdown with the headings A, B, C, D. Do not use web search.
