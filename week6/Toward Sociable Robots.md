

# Toward Sociable Robots

Paper:  Toward sociable robots -- Rating 5/5

Wish:
I think this is a very foundational paper, expressions and interactions of humans to robots will determine if humans are likely to interact with robots. The "interface" from human to robot is absolutely crucial as we tend to like what we know. The paper highlights how we can adapt slightly e.g. the delay of 0.5 seconds in communication. I wish the people who created Furby did read this paper but of course the paper was only released in 2003 while kismet was already around in 1990's

Like:
Eye contact is a very important aspect of Swiss culture when talking, its primary attention seeking initiative, many cultures do the same or have similar behaviors. The direct use of eye contact in this case to hand over the conversation and communicate passively is a very advanced communication pattern. We humans perceive eye to eye view as a sign of respect and attention, giving a human being that feeling through a robot face emulates even with its reduced speech of Kismet the pattern of how babies communicate with their mothers. The paper indicates to me that any future research or robot should pay good attention to the direct eye to eye communication as a very powerful tool to make robots acceptable in daily life.

Like:
The speech protocol given in Table A.1 shows that besides the speech represented as Babble which of course had this reduced set at the time was extremely effective, if we would anonymize the spoken words and Babble i would say it becomes almost unclear that it is a robot to human communication. The table misses the human interaction apart from the eye contact, so through this the difference might be visible. Nevertheless, it is extremely well structured. One thing i noticed is the time, im not entirely sure that this is one conversation or many split ones glued together. The first time code was at 07:13:05 and the last one 20 minutes later, which indicate that it was maybe one of the first good walk troughs or that the robot at the time needed a lot of "Babble" time before it stopped.

Criticism:
With the current information in the paper, there is almost no information to what extend when the robot is angry, sad or happy. It would have been nice to understand if it was all machine-steered through the different processors as this was a 25'000$ machine (at the time much more valuable as today, probably around 3x). It doesn't mention anything in this regard, if it was more like a Wizard of Oz Simulation or if it had a fixed programmed pattern with some randomness. It would have been great to see their actual data in the paper to understand what simple fundamentals they used to really achieve this behaviour and how it could evolve.


Paper:  Slow robots for Unobtrusive Posture Correction -- Rating 5/5

Like:
As we are having more and more people in front of computers, devices and machines it is among the most important and relevant factors. While i've seen that the robotic arm costs 50'000 USD+ per arm and it is not possible to use it yet to support us in business or private applications for this use case, the study itself is super good. So many hours i've spent in front of the computer and only much later you find out why your neck hurt. 

Criticism:
Such an extensive setup might have given the people a clear hint that this is about ergonomics and how the screen with the robotic arm can support it. By doing the prior procedure which was aimed to make the people sit correctly and have the right setup, i believe that this supported the study itself but reinforces the point that people would have to know how to sit correctly prior otherwise this might not work. I do understand to some extent why, looking at the screen doesn't just start with the height of the eyes which the software was tracking but also the sitting, some of the research might be invalid if people don't sit correctly and ergonomically in the first place.

Like:
It is quite surprising that the people found again a balanced posture without any further instructions. I do like that the system was designed to address this intuitively enough that on illustration User Posture (page 5) was already being addressed in maybe 1/3rd of the time the person was sitting in an unbalanced state. So it seems that the movement of the screen has been very effective. The additional information in "Posture Correction" seems to reinforce this even more, as on the second day the sitting time in unbalanced postures was even lower.

Like:
I was looking at the future research section, i think it is important that we consider low-cost solutions in future research which can actually be adopted, it seems to be mentioning the chairs which could become adaptable, a fully integrated system would be great because its not always the upper posture, it start with sitting correctly. While i would say VR  is a big one as when you stand in the world, you might be forced to look left / right a very long time. Which can create neck pain especially if you are wearing a device which has its own weight which the person has to lift. A future research topic which is missing for me altogether is mobile devices. We are daily in bad positions looking down to our hands, some people for 3-7 hours (e.g. Youth Tiktok usage). Those impacts might outpace normal desktop setups, this can also be said for notebooks which traditionally have the screen on a very low level compared to actual desktop screens.
