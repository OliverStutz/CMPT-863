Paper Name: Zooids: Building Blocks for Swarm User Interfaces -- Rating 4/5

  Wish: 

  The idea I like most, zooids as physical pixels or dots, is also where my doubt sits: at 2.6 cm per element, a one meter area, and a unit cost that makes larger swarms (which would be potentially interessting) too expensive. I also can't name a UI driven task which i would prefere to represent with zooids compared to an actual screen rendered visual react/UI, thinking of our llm era even more. The authors say as much: free positioning "comes at the cost of slower response time and higher engineering complexity", and GUIs "currently support far higher information resolution". While the paper claims physical manipulation is a key feature, i can't see this benefit unless 1000's of them in smaller size can assemble an object in realtime or provide a temporary structure which can be held by a hand. When thinking about the whole idea i thought blind people could potentially benefit from this, as braille uses rounded dots to represent letters and numbers but i believe they should be probably 2.6 mm for this, where handling might also be much more difficult.

  Like:
  Reading the hardware section of the paper, i can tell that they tried to reduce the design as much as possible. Similar projects which orchestrate larger bodies of robots like the studies from EPFL in Switzerland, have hardware which is in the megabyte area for ram but equivalent for the cpu too. The sketch also shows that the motors and other hardware has been meticulously placed to save space and reduce size. The 3D printed body also just confirmed that.
  
  Like: 
  Wobbrock, Morris and Wilson's user-defined gestures paper, which I presented in Week 4, maps onto Zooids more cleanly than I expected. Once i read the section in the journal about the gestures it became obvious that the initial paper influenced it but when i checked it wasn't in the citations list. The design space defined also shows that the interaction for gestures can be very wide potentiall already with the 2.6cm version of the zooids. Which would become only more interessting as they shrink in the future.

  Like:
  The part which go my attention more than any text is the picture showing objects to atoms. It is absolutely an inspirational part when you think about the zooids becoming at least particle like where they could represent fluid objects if paired with drone like technologies. The element manipulation would of course become difficult but i believe that this might turn into a more "use" than "manipulate" technology.
