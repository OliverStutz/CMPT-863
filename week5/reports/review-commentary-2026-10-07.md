# Review of Commentaries.txt (Zooids, Week 5), 2026-10-07

Method: facts checked against the PDF text of "Week 5 - Zooids.pdf" (~/Downloads), then two independent Opus reviewers with the same brief. Vote counts are out of 3 (me plus two reviewers). The file was not edited.

## Must fix (3 of 3)

1. No 1-5 rating. The brief requires one. Both reviewers would give 4/5: new interface class, open hardware, candid limits; no user evaluation.
2. Paragraph 3 argues for drones ("I would rather build it in three dimensions"), paragraph 4 ends by endorsing the authors' ground path ("without wings"). Pick one, or turn P3 into a question (can a drone keep on-body touch sensing at a graspable size?).
3. Paragraph 2 (the Wish) lists costs but offers no remedy. End with the missing study: zooids vs a tablet on the time-series range task (Figure 4), measuring task time, recall and engagement; or a niche where screens fail, e.g. vision-impaired users, which the paper cites as a benefit of physicalization (p. 99) and never tests. Use the Figure 4 line chart as the example, not Figure 11: Figure 11 is a Bresenham vs free-positioning illustration, not an application.
4. Wobbrock's terms are misapplied. Signs are the gestures users perform; LED colours are output coding (affordance). Aim the elicitation at the controller zooids (range-slider thumbs, mode switch, timeline zooid, p. 100) with referents such as "widen the time range". Direct displacement needs no elicitation.
5. "The IEEE drone work I linked (…6603259)": unread (Appendix B) and unnamed, and a Canvas reader sees no link. Name the paper or delete the sentence; Kushleyev et al. [33] already carries the point. The document number could not be identified from here.
6. Paragraph 1 reads as a spec summary. Keep two or three numbers that prove the thesis (non-colinear motors, off-board projector, 10-slave radio) and spend the rest on the consequence: the same choices reappear as the limitations on p. 105 (touch only around the rim, projector dependence, manual charging).

## Factual slips (verified against the PDF)

- "small-sized" in quotation marks: the phrase is not in the paper ("small custom-made robots", p. 101). Remove the quotes.
- "two cheap photodiodes": "cheap" is not in the paper.
- "going smaller means … or moving to printable, foldable robots": the paper offers printable/foldable robots as a route to lower cost, not smaller size (p. 105).
- "(p. 101, Figure 6)" for tracking: the paper's text says "demonstrated in Figure 6", but the printed Figure 6 is the software architecture and Figure 7 the PID state machine. Cite p. 101 only.
- "position sensing is pushed off the robot": the photodiodes and decoding stay on the robot; only the projector is off-board, which is exactly why there is no network latency (p. 101). Rephrase.
- "last one hour on a 100 mAh battery": one hour of motion, "even longer under normal usage" (p. 101).
- "fails under occlusion and ambient light": paper says "limited by occlusions" and "hard to use with too much ambient light" (p. 105). Soften.
- "matter that forms itself": Zooids "employ a centralized system to coordinate robots" (p. 99). Say "matter a computer can form".
- Everything else checked out: all other numbers, direct quotes and page numbers.

## Fit and form

- Length: 922 words, 56 sentences (13/19/13/11) against roughly 4 sentences per point. Target about 6 per paragraph.
- Paragraph 3 makes two points (elicitation, then 3D). Keep one.
- Canvas: "##" headings and "---" rules will show literally; the title em-dash, "×" and "–" may garble. Use plain labels ("Like 1:", "Wish:") and put the Wish last.
- Tangible Bits (Ishii & Ullmer 1997) is listed in the header but never used. One sentence in P4 (Tangible Bits → Radical Atoms → Zooids) covers "connections to other readings". Check whether the thread also wants a rating for Tangible Bits ("rate each paper").

- Wording: "I can tell that", "the whole part arriving at", "nothing I would prefer over a laptop" (dismissive). P2 "The concept I liked most" competes with P4 "What I find most exciting".
