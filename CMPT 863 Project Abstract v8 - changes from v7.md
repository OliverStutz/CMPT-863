# Abstract v8: changes from v7 (2026-10-19)

Made after the reading pass (`Project Literature Review/search/verified-view.md`, rules 1 to 22) and the three-reviewer novelty check (`Project Literature Review/search/novelty-tally.md`). The study itself is unchanged: same groups, slides, flags, answers and ratings.

1. **Research question.** One contribution paragraph added: prior work shows the AI-disagreement penalty with a silent expert (Madanay 2025, Chen 2026) and the reply's effect after a human accusation (Kim 2004, Ferrin 2007); we combine the two with the checker in the hands of the party with power.
2. **Trust definition.** The 0-to-10 rating plus one item each for competence, benevolence and integrity (Mayer et al. 1995; wording from METI, Hendriks et al. 2015). The "Review impact" note in Measures is folded into this (rules 1 and 6).
3. **H2.** "Higher than after the flag, and higher than in the neutral group"; "above the first rating" is now exploratory (reviewers and Greitemeyer 2015).
4. **H3.** "Rises less than after a reasoned answer, or falls further" instead of "lower than after the flag" (rule 2; Kim 2004 and Ferrin 2007 cannot say whether a bare reply lowers trust further).
5. **H4b.** Marked exploratory (Kim 2026's null on AI involvement).
6. **Method.** Allocation sentence rewritten for clarity (round robin kept). Budget stakes identical and visible for both groups (rule 13). Flags worded about the slide, never about honesty; no accuracy or confidence figure on the panel (rules 17 and 20). Reasoned answers and the denial delivered by the same CISO in the same tone (rules 8 and 15).
7. **Measures.** Self-rated cybersecurity knowledge and own AI use named as planned moderators of H1 and H2 (rules 19 and 20).
8. **Validity.** Two limits added: no correct-flag condition, so wrong flags are compared with no flags; participants learn the flags were wrong only at the debrief (novelty check, hostile case).
9. **Appendix.** Kim et al. (2026) replaces Chen et al. (2026) as the closest design, with Madanay et al. (2025) added; Chen kept as close on a different axis (rule 22). New paragraph on Kim et al. (2004) and Ferrin et al. (2007) as the trust-repair precedent for H2 and H3.
10. **References.** Seven added: Ferrin 2007, Hendriks 2015, Kempt & Nagel 2022, Kim 2026, Kim 2004, Madanay 2025, Mayer 1995. Total 15.
11. **Two corrections from the structure reviewers (2026-10-19, checked against full texts).** The burden-of-proof point is Kempt and Nagel's (2022), which Chen et al. (2026) cite; v7 attributed it to Chen. Madanay's undercalling radiologist was "rated lowest of the four conditions", not "the worst provider".
12. **One correction from the fact check (2026-10-19, `Project Literature Review/search/fact-check-2026-10-19.md`).** Kim et al. (2026) removed from the citation for "an AI's disagreement lowers trust in a human expert when the expert stays silent" in the research question: in Kim et al. the advisor's error lowered trust and how the AI was involved changed nothing, so the paper bounds that claim rather than supporting it. Kim et al. stay as the closest design in the appendix.

Not changed, for Oliver to decide: round-robin allocation (all three novelty reviewers asked for random assignment or a stated reason); recruitment of lay volunteers to role-play directors (reviewers suggest recruiting people with board, audit or oversight experience where possible); the "No issues found" wording on slides 1 and 5 versus a supporting label (rule 16 and paper 18).
