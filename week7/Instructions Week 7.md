Each student will **co-lead a class on two days during the term**. Two students will work together to present the assigned papers and facilitate discussion.

For each paper, plan for approximately:

- **30 minutes: Presentation**
- **30 minutes: Discussion**

The goal is not simply to summarize the readings, but to help the class understand the paper’s contribution, evaluate it critically, and engage with its broader implications.

#### Presentation

Your presentation should give the class enough background to understand the research while focusing primarily on the paper’s **ideas, contributions, methods, and implications**. Aim to address:

- What problem does the paper address, and why does it matter?
- What is the paper’s main idea or contribution?
- How was the research conducted?
- What are the most important findings or takeaways?
- What are the paper’s strengths and limitations?
- How does it connect to other research or topics we have discussed?
- What new questions or research directions does it suggest?

Avoid a lengthy section-by-section summary. Assume that everyone has completed the reading, and use the presentation to surface the aspects of the paper that are most important for understanding and discussing it.

#### Leading the Discussion

The second half of each paper session should be a **conversation, not an additional lecture**. Come prepared with a set of questions, tensions, or activities that encourage students to analyze the paper and build on one another’s ideas.

Strong discussions often:

- Move between **high-level questions** about the paper’s significance and **specific questions** about its methods, design, or evidence.
- Ask questions that allow for multiple defensible perspectives rather than simple factual answers.
- Use brief pair or small-group discussions when useful to get more students involved.
- Follow interesting ideas that emerge rather than rigidly working through a list of prepared questions.
- Encourage participation across the class rather than allowing a few students to dominate.

Your role is to **facilitate**: keep the discussion moving, probe interesting responses, ask follow-up questions, and redirect when the conversation moves too far from the topic.

#### Using Student Commentaries

Presenters will have access to classmates’ paper commentaries before class (reply anything to the discussion thread and you'll have access). Read them carefully and use them to inform your discussion. Look for recurring questions, disagreements, critiques, interesting connections, and particularly insightful observations. We strongly encourage you to incorporate these ideas into your slides and discussion, **crediting the students who raised them**.

Presenters **do not need to submit commentaries for the days they are presenting**.

#### Grading Commentaries and Participation

The two discussion co-leads will also help evaluate their classmates’ engagement that day. After class, you will provide:

- **Commentary grades** based primarily on the depth and quality of students’ intellectual engagement with the readings.
- **Discussion participation grades** based on students’ preparation and meaningful contributions to the conversation.

You should complete these evaluations shortly after class, while the discussion is still fresh. Course staff will provide the grading criteria and submission process.
