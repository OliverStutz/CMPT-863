## Slide 17: What the paper changed, 2012 to 2016: three lanes

**Time:** 1:30 · **Visual:** three columns side by side, headed "Gaver's way," "The critics' way" and "Both," each a short list of items with years; a small dagger after each item that cites the essay. A strip along the bottom in small type in the second colour: the "our labels" note, the dagger legend and the citation count. No figure.
**Images:** none
**Build:** one column per click, left to right; the bottom strip with the last column.

**On the slide**
- **Gaver's way:** Gaver and Bowers 2012; Bowers 2012†; RTD conference 2013; DIS Pictorials 2014; Pierce 2014†
- **The critics' way:** strong concepts 2012†; bridging concepts 2014; Zimmerman and Forlizzi 2014; Bardzell et al. 2016†
- **Both:** Löwgren 2013†, intermediate-level knowledge; one 2014 book prints both sides
- Lane names are ours. † cites the essay. About 1,100 citations (OpenAlex, 2026).

**Speaker notes**
- **Say:** Right after CHI, Gaver and John Bowers published a short piece called "Annotated Portfolios," and Bowers argued its logic at DIS that summer. Both come from Gaver's own studio, so that is continuation, not uptake. The independent signs came next. A Research Through Design conference started in 2013 that exhibits the artefacts and discusses them at round tables. DIS added Pictorials in 2014, papers where the images carry the argument. Pierce proposed "thingly publications." We argue the form found venues, not a rule. [click] The second lane, and the lane names are ours, kept moving toward knowledge above single designs: strong concepts from Höök and Löwgren, bridging concepts from Dalsgaard and Dindler, and a 2016 framework for documenting the research through design process. Zimmerman and Forlizzi wrote in 2014 that they had worked to formalize the approach. [click] And in the middle, Löwgren filed annotated portfolios as one kind of intermediate-level knowledge. One 2014 book prints both sides. OpenAlex now counts about eleven hundred citations to the essay. (about 184 words)
- **Transition:** That was the first four years. Fourteen years on, the clearest sign is at CHI itself.
- **Watch out:**
  - Lane labels are ours (P10). "The critics' way" does not mean these authors opposed Gaver; Höök and Löwgren 2012 cite the essay. Say "toward generalisable or formalised knowledge," and do not call anyone a positivist.
  - Gaver and Bowers 2012 (Interactions, July 2012) and Bowers 2012 (DIS, June 2012) come from Gaver's own studio: continuation, not uptake. The independent evidence in his lane is the RTD conference, DIS Pictorials and Pierce 2014. No dagger on Gaver and Bowers: its full text is restricted, so its reference list was not checked.
  - Daggers, from Crossref and OpenAlex reference lists (`../slides/10-What-Followed.md`): Höök and Löwgren 2012, Bowers 2012, Löwgren 2013, Pierce 2014 and Bardzell et al. 2016 cite the essay. Dalsgaard and Dindler 2014: absent from the deposited list, so we claim no link. Zimmerman and Forlizzi 2014 do not cite it (their reference list, pp. 187 to 189); they say they "worked to formalize this research approach" (p. 178). Neither rebuttal nor concession.
  - Höök and Löwgren's strong concepts appeared in TOCHI in October 2012, after CHI 2012 in May: a parallel answer from the same year, not something Gaver ignored.
  - Bridging concepts could sit in "both": the abstract asks for "a theoretical foundation, a set of design articulations and a range of exemplars." If challenged, concede the move.
  - Bardzell et al. 2016 is "a framework for planning and evaluating RtD documentation," which speaks to the 2010 call for protocols (p. 937) without asking design to become a science (P2).
  - "About 1,100": OpenAlex gave 1,139 on 2026-10-19. Keep it approximate.
- **If asked:**
  - *Q: What is a strong concept?* A: Höök and Löwgren's intermediate-level knowledge, "more abstracted than particular instances, without aspiring to be at the scope of generalized theories." Examples: social navigation, seamfulness. Judged as "contestable, defensible, and substantive."
  - *Q: What is a Pictorial?* A: A DIS paper format from 2014 in which visuals lead. The DIS 2026 call: "In papers images illustrate the text, while in pictorials text is used to annotate the visuals." Twelve pages excluding references, dual anonymous review, the same quality as papers.
  - *Q: What did the 2014 book say?* A: Ways of Knowing in HCI (Olson and Kellogg, eds.) prints Zimmerman and Forlizzi's chapter, with five steps: select, design, evaluate, reflect and disseminate, repeat (p. 184), and Gaver's chapter, which says "Annotated portfolios may capture best what we can know through design" (p. 161).
  - *Q: Did Löwgren and Bowers agree on what annotations are?* A: No. Löwgren reports that Bowers sees annotations as "purely indexical and inseparable from their particular artifacts," while Löwgren reads them as abstractions with a wider scope. Even friendly readers disagreed within a year.
  - *Q: What did Gaver and Bowers's own portfolio show?* A: As Löwgren summarises it, two pieces for older people with annotations such as "influencing autonomous drift," then three earlier studio pieces added. That is the worked studio portfolio the essay only sketched with Rams.

## Slide 18: What is it worth in 2026?

**Time:** 1:30 · **Visual:** four short rows, each a bold label in our colour and one line; the generative AI row carries an "our question" tag in the second colour. Pull quote across the bottom. No figure.
**Images:** none
**Build:** one row per click; the quote with the generative AI row.

**On the slide**
- **CHI 2026:** Design subcommittee invites "alternatives to scientistic ways of knowing." Scope, not a reviewing rule.
- **RTD Roams, 2027:** York, Melbourne, Tokyo. Pictorials at DIS, TEI, Creativity and Cognition.
- **Generative AI (our question):** outputs by the hundred. What is one object worth?
- **Cost:** do portfolios favour funded labs?
- Quote: "the definite nature of designed artefacts" (Gaver, p. 943)

**Speaker notes**
- **Say:** CHI 2026. The Design subcommittee now invites work exploring "alternatives to scientistic ways of knowing." That is close to Gaver's diagnosis. But it is a scope statement, not reviewing practice. Design drew 485 submissions and moved on to revise and resubmit at about the overall rate, just under two in five. The door is open; whether reviewing changed, we cannot say. [click] The Research Through Design conference paused after 2019 and returns in 2027 as RTD Roams, in York, Melbourne and Tokyo. Pictorials spread from DIS to TEI and Creativity and Cognition. [click] Now a question of ours. Suh and colleagues note that language models can produce "hundreds and thousands" of outputs, yet current interfaces push users "towards rapid convergence on a limited set of ideas." Davis and colleagues found graphical design-space interfaces supported ideation better than text prompts. Neither paper is about research through design. But Gaver rests his case on the definite nature of designed artefacts. What is one definite object worth when variants are nearly free? [click] And the reverse: built portfolios are expensive. Do they favour funded labs? (about 185 words)
- **Transition:** Some of this was already in our own course readings, starting with Tangible Bits.
- **Watch out:**
  - CHI 2026 Design subcommittee text (`../slides/10-What-Followed.md`): it invites "suggestions and provocations exploring new design tools, processes, methods, or principles, including those that explore alternatives to scientistic ways of knowing." A scope statement, not reviewing practice (P10). Gaver's word on p. 938 is "scientism"; say "close to his diagnosis," not "his words."
  - The numbers: eighteen subcommittees, 6,730 paper submissions; Design 485, with 37.9 percent moving to revise and resubmit against 38.7 percent overall (https://chi2026.acm.org/2025/12/12/insights-into-the-papers-track-first-round-outcomes/). First-round outcomes only.
  - RTD ran 2013 (the BALTIC in Gateshead), 2015 (Cambridge), 2017 (Edinburgh), 2019 (Delft and Rotterdam), paused after 2019, and returns in 2027 as "RTD Roams" in York, Melbourne (Naarm) and Tokyo, announced on 23 July 2026 (https://researchthroughdesign.org/announcing-rtd-roams/). If someone says it stopped, concede the pause.
  - Pictorial tracks: introduced at DIS 2014; TEI (2020 call) and Creativity and Cognition (2023 call) also run them. We claim a venue for the form, not Gaver's influence on it.
  - The generative AI link is ours (P10). Suh et al. (Luminate, CHI 2024) and Davis et al. (CHI 2024) are not about research through design. Keep "hundreds and thousands" as their phrase and the number vague.
  - "The definite nature of designed artefacts" (p. 943) is in Gaver's explanation of why designers turn to examples: "both because of the provisional nature of theory, and the definite nature of designed artefacts."
  - Cost: the grant is acknowledged on p. 946. That portfolios favour such labs is our question, never Gaver's motive. Slide 15 raised it; keep this to one sentence.
- **If asked:**
  - *Q: So did CHI reviewing move toward Gaver?* A: We cannot tell. In 2014 Zimmerman and Forlizzi reported "two technical papers committees devoted to design research" (Ways of Knowing in HCI, p. 178). In 2026 the Design subcommittee's scope names "alternatives to scientistic ways of knowing." Scope, not practice.
  - *Q: What did Davis et al. actually compare?* A: Their title states it: "Graphical Interfaces for Design Space Exploration Better Support Ideation Than Text Prompts" (CHI 2024, https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145/3613904.3642908).
  - *Q: Does cheap generation help or hurt Gaver?* A: Both readings work. Help: many worlds become cheap to explore, which fits "multiple, incompatible worlds" (p. 943). Hurt: if the artefact is "the definite facts of research through design" (p. 945), a flood of variants makes any one artefact less definite. That is the discussion's question, not a finding.
  - *Q: How big was Gaver's grant?* A: ERC 226528, ThirdWaveHCI: 2,439,757 euros, 2009 to 2014, aiming to deploy 50 to 100 batch-produced prototypes (https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/226528, via `../slides/10-What-Followed.md`).

## Slide 19: Where this touches what we have read

**Time:** 1:30 · **Visual:** four cards in a two-by-two grid. Each card: the course reading in bold, one plain line, and a small tag in the corner: "instance," "same diagnosis," "counter," "half right." Footer line in small type: "Gaver cites none; every link is ours." No figure.
**Images:** none
**Build:** one card per click, footer with the first card.

**On the slide**
- **Tangible Bits, 1997:** one metaphor, three platforms. A portfolio before the name.
- **Greenberg and Buxton, 2008:** same diagnosis; refutation kept for the mainstream.
- **Wobbrock, Morris and Wilson, 2009:** their method, 216 studies by 2020. Fertile, and still science.
- **Shin et al., 2019:** realistic, yes; unambiguous, no. Gaver half right.
- Gaver cites none; every link is ours.

**Speaker notes**
- **Say:** Gaver cites none of our readings, so every link here is ours. Tangible Bits. Ishii and Ullmer join three platforms, the metaDESK, the ambientROOM and the transBOARD, with one optical metaphor, and write about what holds them together. We think that is the clearest annotated portfolio we have read, fifteen years before Gaver named the form. [click] Greenberg and Buxton make Gaver's Popper diagnosis for HCI evaluation: our studies look for confirmation. Their cure keeps refutation for the mainstream. Gaver drops it for design theory. [click] Wobbrock, Morris and Wilson used an elicitation method that dates from 2005. By 2020 a review counted 216 studies using it. By Gaver's fertility test that is a thriving programme, and it is ordinary empirical science. Our point: fertility rescues design theory without setting design apart. [click] Shin and colleagues in week six tested a slow robot for four hours in a realistic setting. Gaver said a test both unambiguous and true to life is inconceivable. Shin got the true-to-life half. But actuation always came on day two, and eleven of twelve participants guessed the purpose. Unambiguous, no. We say Gaver is half right. (about 193 words)
- **Transition:** One last look at the object we started with.
- **Watch out:**
  - Ishii and Ullmer: "one user interface metaphor that can be applied seamlessly across the metaDESK, ambientROOM, and transBOARD platforms" (their p. 240). The "portfolio before the name" reading is ours. They concede the metaphor is "somewhat weaker" on the transBOARD (p. 240).
  - Greenberg and Buxton: "the evaluator is seeking confirmatory evidence" (their p. 113); the split cure is their p. 118. Gaver's matching diagnosis: "practice is usually intended to confirm theories, not falsify them" (p. 940). They evaluate systems, Gaver discusses design theory: say "same diagnosis," not "same argument." Gaver does not drop evaluation; he drops refutation as the test for design theory.
  - Wobbrock, Morris and Wilson (P12): the method dates from Wobbrock et al. 2005; the 216 count is from the DIS 2020 review by Villarreal-Narvaez, Vanderdonckt, Vatavu and Wobbrock (p. 855). Never "follow-up studies" of the 2009 paper. Gaver uses fertility only to rescue design theory from Popper (p. 941); our point is that the test does not pick out design.
  - Shin et al. (P11): "ecological validity" (their p. 5); actuation only on day 2 (p. 5); "all of the participants guessed that the monitor was trying to correct their posture, except P2" (p. 7). Gaver's sentence: tests "which are simultaneously unambiguous and ecologically valid are inconceivable" (p. 940). "Half right," never "refuted." The instructions also pointed to health (their p. 6), so blame "the instructions and the setup."
  - Gaver's reference list includes none of these readings (p. 946).
- **If asked:**
  - *Q: Why is Tangible Bits a portfolio and not just three systems?* A: The paper articulates what joins them (the optical metaphor) and what separates them (desk, room, board), with the systems kept particular. That is Gaver's balance, "the ideas and issues that join and differentiate them" (p. 945). The authors did not call it that.
  - *Q: Isn't Greenberg and Buxton's cure the more careful one?* A: We argue so. Existence proofs for the new is close to Gaver's "ultimate particular," but they keep refutation for mainstream work, where Gaver's argument covers all design theory.
  - *Q: Isn't the Wobbrock row a straw man, since Gaver never says fertility separates design from science?* A: Fair. Our point is narrower: an empirical line passes the fertility test just as Gaver describes it, so the test shows a programme is alive, not which kind of knowledge produced it.
  - *Q: Any other readings that fit?* A: Two alternates. Olsen 2007: three diverse solutions support a claim about "the space between" them; Gaver's portfolio "establishes an area" (p. 944), and Olsen states what Gaver only sketches, that more diverse examples make a stronger claim. Wobbrock and Kientz 2016: "wary of constraining our imaginations," yet "embrace giving definition to the knowledge we produce" (their p. 44); Gaver is "wary of impulses towards convergence and standardisation" (p. 937). The debate in one word.
