## Slide 6: Research through design was being done by building things and writing theory around them.

**Time:** 1:00 · **Visual:** two columns. Left, "Build it, live with it": the pull quote on top and two small thumbnail captions (Drift Table, Prayer Companion) with their durations. Right, "Five kinds of theory": a stacked list of five rows, each a bold name and one example, with meta-theory at the bottom in our colour.
**Images:** none
**Build:** left column first; [click] the five rows at once.

**On the slide**
- Quote: built things, "sometimes with associated accounts of how these are used in field tests" (Gaver, p. 937)
- Drift Table: weeks in homes. Prayer Companion: ten months with nuns.
- **Five kinds of theory:**
  - Articulations of practice: embodied movement
  - Borrowed theory: affordances become "irresistables" (sic)
  - Manifestos: Reflective Design, ludic design
  - Frameworks: Product Ecology
  - Meta-theory: theory about the approach, this essay included

**Speaker notes**
- **Say:** How was research through design being done? First, you build something. Gaver says the output is primarily artefacts and systems, sometimes with accounts of how they are used in field tests. From other publications by Gaver and colleagues: the Drift Table was lived with for several weeks, and the Prayer Companion stayed with a convent of nuns for ten months. [click] Second, people wrote theory around the things. Gaver sorts it into five kinds. Articulations of practice, like Djajadiningrat and colleagues on embodied movement, built from reflecting on their own designs. Borrowed theory, like affordances, which Overbeeke and Wensveen turned into irresistables. Manifestos, as on slide four. Frameworks, like Product Ecology. And meta-theory, theory about research through design itself, where Gaver puts Zimmerman and Forlizzi, Stolterman, and this essay. (about 125 words)
- **Transition:** So that is the work. How did people propose to judge it? In 2007, with four lenses.
- **Watch out:**
  - Neither the Drift Table nor the Prayer Companion is in Gaver's paper. Say "from other publications by Gaver and colleagues" (P1). The Prayer Companion is CHI 2010, from Goldsmiths.
  - Gaver also says the output "increasingly includes a variety of methods, conceptual frameworks and theories presented separately from accounts of practice" (p. 937). That sentence is the bridge between the two columns if you need one.
  - "'irresistables'" is the paper's in-text spelling (p. 938); the reference title spells "irresistibles" (p. 946, ref. 23). A slip in the paper (P9); mention only if asked.
  - The five kinds are Gaver's grouping, not an established taxonomy. He calls meta-theory "A final genre" (p. 938), and says this essay "is intended as a contribution to meta-theory" (p. 939) but addresses the "more specific, content-oriented theories" (p. 939).
  - Meta-theory attribution: the in-text "Zimmerman & Forlizzi [34]" (pp. 938 to 939) points to the 2007 paper, but the phrases quoted there are from the 2008 paper [33]. Say "Zimmerman and Forlizzi" without a year.
- **If asked:**
  - *Q: Why call manifestos and frameworks "theory"?* A: Gaver uses a broad sense: when conceptual statements "are articulated in general terms and applied to multiple examples, they become recognisable as theories in their own right" (p. 938). Product Ecology "implies a conceptual orientation" (p. 938).
  - *Q: What is the embodied movement work?* A: Djajadiningrat, Matthews and Stienstra 2007 [6] develop "a rich account of the importance of embodied movement" from "a series of designs they and their students have produced" (p. 938).
  - *Q: What did the Prayer Companion do?* A: A device for a group of cloistered nuns showing a stream of news and social networking items as possible topics for prayer; after ten months the nuns said it had a meaningful, lasting role in their prayer (CHI 2010; `../slides/01-Background.md` background item 3).
  - *Q: What is Product Ecology concretely?* A: Forlizzi 2008 frames a product inside the product, other products, people, place, and social and cultural context, illustrated with households given a Roomba (`../slides/04-Theory.md`, If asked).
- **Sources:** paper p. 937 (output, field tests, "increasingly includes"), p. 938 (articulations of practice, borrowed theory, "'irresistables'", manifestos, frameworks, meta-theory begins), p. 939 (meta-theory, "contribution to meta-theory"), p. 946 (refs 6, 10, 23). `../slides/04-Theory.md` slide 04.1 and notes; `../slides/01-Background.md` background item 3 and log.

## Slide 7: People proposed to judge it by four criteria, then by protocols and metrics.

**Time:** 1:00 · **Visual:** four cards in a row, Process, Invention, Relevance, Extensibility, each with a one-line gloss; Relevance carries a small strike-through "validity" above it. Below the cards, two short lines for 2010 and 2011. A closing line in our colour across the bottom.
**Images:** none
**Build:** the four cards at once; [click] the 2010 and 2011 lines and the closing line.

**On the slide**
- **Zimmerman, Forlizzi and Evenson 2007, four lenses:**
  - Process: rigour and a documented rationale; reproducible, without expecting the same result
  - Invention: a new integration, shown against the literature
  - Relevance: in place of validity
  - Extensibility: others can build on it
- 2010: towards a formalisation
- 2011: "actionable metrics"
- **These are the proposals Gaver answers.**

**Speaker notes**
- **Say:** In 2007 Zimmerman, Forlizzi and Evenson proposed four lenses for judging a research through design contribution. Process: the methods need rigour and a rationale, documented so others could reproduce them, with no expectation of getting the same result. Invention: a new integration of ideas, shown against the literature. Relevance, which they put in place of validity. In their words, instead of validity, the benchmark should be relevance. And extensibility: others can build on the work. [click] Then the push grew. The 2010 paper set out towards a formalisation of the approach. The 2011 panel wanted actionable metrics. These are the proposals Gaver answers. (about 103 words)
- **Transition:** Gaver's response to these proposals begins with a concession.
- **Watch out:**
  - The glosses are paraphrases of the 2007 paper (pp. 499 to 500); none is in quotation marks on the slide. The exact wording for Process is "no expectation that reproducing the process will produce the same results" (2007, p. 499); do not quote the shorter form.
  - "Instead of validity, the benchmark for interaction design research should be relevance" (2007, p. 499) is the exact sentence; the Say shortens it after "benchmark", so say "in their words" only for "instead of validity".
  - The 2010 paper's subtitle is "toward a formalization of a research approach" in Gaver's reference list (p. 946) and "towards a formalization" on Crossref. Do not put it in quotation marks.
  - "Not asking for laboratory science" is our reading (P2), supported by the relevance lens.
  - Do not repeat slide 5's quotations in full; the 2010 and 2011 lines point back to it.
- **If asked:**
  - *Q: What was the 2007 model behind the lenses?* A: Designers integrate "true" knowledge from behavioural scientists, "how" knowledge from engineers and "real" knowledge from anthropologists, to make "the right thing", moving the world to a "preferred state" (2007, pp. 493, 497). Gaver borrows "the 'right thing'" on p. 940.
  - *Q: Did the 2007 authors claim to invent research through design?* A: No: "the idea is not new within the HCI and interaction design research community" (2007, p. 499). They named it and proposed criteria.
  - *Q: What examples did they use?* A: Xerox copier interfaces, Philips' Vision of the Future, and Apple Guides (2007, pp. 500 to 501).
  - *Q: Does Gaver reject extensibility?* A: No. He later claims annotated portfolios allow "for the 'extensibility and verifiability' for which some of the HCI design community have called" (p. 945).
- **Sources:** `../slides/01-Background.md` background item 2 and log (Zimmerman et al. 2007, pp. 493 to 501: https://courses.ischool.berkeley.edu/i262/s13/readings_pdf/Zimmerman_Research_Through_Design_as_a_Method_for_IaD_in_HCI_0.pdf; https://doi.org/10.1145/1240624.1240704); `../slides/02-Problem.md` log row 77; paper p. 937 ([9], [35]), p. 940, p. 945, p. 946 (ref. 35).

## Slide 8: Gaver objects: standards risk self-policing and scientism.

**Time:** 1:15 · **Visual:** the concession in a small line at the top. Two boxes side by side, "Risk 1: self-policing" (the pull quote) and "Risk 2: scientism" (Gaver's fragments). A thin footer strip with reviewer R4's line. Bottom right, in the second colour, the "why you care" line marked "ours".
**Images:** none
**Build:** concession first; [click] the two risks; [click] R4 and the "why you care" line.

**On the slide**
- "some sympathy with these concerns"
- Quote: standards "might lead to a form of self-policing that would be overly restrictive" (Gaver, p. 937)
- "insofar as HCI is prone to 'scientism'", standards "may adopt, or be interpreted in terms of, inappropriate 'scientific' models"
- Reviewer R4: "an elephant in the room"
- **Why you care (ours):** "Where is your evaluation?" is this fight.

**Speaker notes**
- **Say:** As a long-term practitioner of research through design, he writes, "I have some sympathy with these concerns." [click] Then he names two risks. First, standards "might lead to a form of self-policing that would be overly restrictive" of research he values for challenging status quo thinking. Second, and we think this is the sharper point: insofar as HCI is prone to scientism, standards "may adopt, or be interpreted in terms of, inappropriate 'scientific' models." Listen to the hedges: might, may, insofar. He targets a risk and a reading, not a claim the critics made. [click] One reviewer, R4, wrote that the essay addresses, in part, "an elephant in the room": the move towards scientism in HCI. Gaver prints that in his acknowledgements. Why should you care? This part is ours. "Where is your evaluation?" is this fight in four words. You will hear it about anything you build. (about 155 words)
- **Transition:** How does Gaver argue back? Not with a study: there is no data in this paper. He argues as a long-term practitioner, with philosophy and sociology of science as his tools, and one sketched example.
- **Watch out:**
  - Pitfall P15. Keep every hedge: "some sympathy" (p. 937), "might lead to" (p. 937), "insofar as" and "may adopt, or be interpreted in terms of" (p. 938). He does not say standards are bad.
  - Pitfall P2. The critics did not ask for a science. "Scientism" is Gaver's word for a tendency in HCI's "cultural assumptions" (p. 938), not a label for Zimmerman, Stolterman or Forlizzi.
  - Pitfall P18. R4's full line: "an elephant in the room...: the move towards "scientism" in HCI" (p. 946); the essay addresses it "in part". Gaver cites Sengers 2006, "Must design become 'scientific'?" (p. 941), so he names scientism in a CHI paper; he is not the first to raise it.
  - "Scientism" and "scientific" carry inner single quotes in the paper (p. 938); keep them on the slide; in speech, just say the words.
  - The "why you care" line is ours, not Gaver's. Say "ours" aloud.
  - Do not state why the critics wanted standards; the only reason on record is Gaver's hedged "may be a route towards disciplinary legitimacy" (p. 937), already on slide 5.
- **If asked:**
  - *Q: Does Gaver define scientism?* A: No. The nearest he comes is p. 941: taking disagreements about science seriously "destabilises the use of 'science' as shorthand for a set of practices and criteria assumed to be desirable." We read scientism here as using "science" as a stand-in for good research; that reading is ours.
  - *Q: What does he value that self-policing would restrict?* A: Research "that I value for its ability to continually and creatively challenge status quo thinking" (pp. 937 to 938).
  - *Q: Where does Gaver himself stand on design and science?* A: "Personally, I tend to think that the nature of design practice and research is distinct from the ideals, and often practice, of science", but whether it is a science "depends on the account of science one adopts" (both p. 941, P16).
  - *Q: Did anyone call for policing?* A: Gaver says only that standards "might lead to" it (p. 937). Markussen, Krogh and Bang (2015) later read Zimmerman et al. as calling for "a policing of the research through design label"; Jeffrey Bardzell (2016) answered that he could not see such a call (`../slides/02-Problem.md`, If asked).
  - *Q: Who was R4?* A: Anonymous. Gaver thanks "the anonymous R4" in the acknowledgements (p. 946).
- **Sources:** paper p. 937 ("long-term practitioner", "some sympathy", "self-policing", "disciplinary legitimacy"), p. 938 ("value for its ability", "insofar as", "may adopt, or be interpreted in terms of"), p. 941 (Sengers [29], "shorthand", "Personally"), p. 946 (R4). `../slides2/02-Fight.md` slide 02.1; `../slides/02-Problem.md` slide 02.1 and log.
