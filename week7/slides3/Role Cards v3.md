# Role Cards v3: five pairs for the discussion

**Purpose:** the role-card pack for step 2 on slide 22. Each card gives a pair one question, two roles and one object, enough for five minutes of argument without Oliver's help. The facilitator notes are for Oliver: how to call on the pair, how to push the argument, and what a strong report sounds like.

**How to print:** one card per page, the front on one side and the back on the other. The facilitator notes and the facilitator summary go on separate sheets for Oliver only; do not hand them out. Print each card twice if more than ten students attend (two pairs per card).

**Conventions:** "Gaver" marks Gaver's own words; "quoted in Gaver" marks words he quotes from others; the critics' papers are cited by their own pages; "our reading" marks a claim that is ours, not the text's. Pages: Gaver 2012, CHI, pp. 937 to 946; Zimmerman, Forlizzi and Evenson 2007 (CHI, pp. 493 to 502); Ishii and Ullmer 1997 (CHI, pp. 234 to 241); Frayling 1993 (RCA Research Papers 1(1)). Slide numbers refer to the v3 deck.

## Card 1. Gaver v Zimmerman

### Front

**Card 1: Gaver v Zimmerman**
- **Question:** Should research through design have standards a reviewer can apply?
- **Roles:** A, Gaver (2012). B, Zimmerman (2007, 2010).
- **Object:** the Drift Table (CHI 2004): aerial photographs drift as you lean on a coffee table.
- **Rule:** five minutes, then report in one sentence each: what you argued and the other side's best point.

### Back: Role A, Gaver

- Standards may bring "disciplinary legitimacy", but risk "self-policing" that is "overly restrictive" (Gaver, pp. 937 to 938).
- "Insofar as HCI is prone to 'scientism'", standards may import scientific models (Gaver, p. 938).
- Moderate expectations (Gaver, p. 945): design theories are "not falsifiable in principle" (p. 940), Zimmerman's Designing for the Self among them (pp. 940 to 941).
- **Do not say:** "Gaver rejects all standards." He has "some sympathy with these concerns" (Gaver, p. 937).
- **If stuck:** which of the four criteria would help the Drift Table?

### Back: Role B, Zimmerman

- Four criteria exist: process, invention, relevance, extensibility (Zimmerman et al. 2007, pp. 499 to 500). Apply them.
- We called it "a well known example of critical design in HCI" (2007, p. 496); criteria say why.
- We want "protocols, descriptions, and guidelines" (Zimmerman et al. 2010, quoted in Gaver, p. 937); "Instead of validity", relevance (2007, p. 499).
- **Do not say:** "design should be a science." 2007: "no expectation that reproducing the process will produce the same results" (p. 499).
- **If stuck:** what must a Drift Table paper contain?

### Facilitator note (Oliver only)

- **Call:** "Gaver and Zimmerman: who would accept the Drift Table, and on what terms?"
- **Zoom in:** the critics asked for "actionable metrics" (CHI 2011 panel, quoted in Gaver, p. 937). Name one metric for the Drift Table.
- **Zoom out:** who writes a standard, and against whom is it later used? Bridge to anchor 1.
- **Strong report:** a page from Gaver or the 2007 paper; slide 7 (four criteria) or slide 8 (self-policing); concedes that Zimmerman wants relevance, not validity; applies one criterion to their own project.
- **Trap:** the Zimmerman player defending "design as science". Point to p. 499 of the 2007 paper. Also watch for agreement in thirty seconds ("both are right"); ask which criterion they would drop.

## Card 2. Reviewer v author

### Front

**Card 2: Reviewer v author**
- **Question:** Should CHI accept this annotated portfolio as a paper?
- **Roles:** A, a CHI reviewer. B, the author.
- **Object:** Tangible Bits (1997) as an annotated portfolio: three platforms, one optical metaphor.
- **Rule:** five minutes, then report in one sentence each: what you argued and the other side's best point.

### Back: Role A, reviewer

- I must be able to say no. Extensibility: work "described and documented in a way that the community can leverage the knowledge derived from the work" (Zimmerman et al. 2007, p. 500).
- The CHI 2011 panel wanted "actionable metrics" (quoted in Gaver, p. 937).
- "The notion of annotated portfolios is not a formal one" (Gaver, p. 944). What do I check?
- **Do not say:** "a portfolio is a gallery." Gaver: "neither dominates, and neither is subservient" (p. 945).
- **If stuck:** what sentence would let you reject it?

### Back: Role B, author

- Gaver asks for portfolios "as a serious form of theoretical contribution" (Gaver, p. 945).
- A portfolio "establishes an area" and shows "the dimensions along which a designer's choices may range and the invariances among them" (Gaver, p. 944). Our invariant: the optical metaphor (p. 240; our reading).
- Portfolios allow the "'extensibility and verifiability'" critics called for (Gaver, p. 945).
- **Do not say:** "portfolios replace papers." Gaver: not to "subsume all other forms of design theory" (p. 945).
- **If stuck:** where do annotation and artefact check each other?

### Facilitator note (Oliver only)

- **Call:** "The reviewer and the author: who won? Accept, reject, or revise?"
- **Zoom in:** the authors themselves write that "On the transBOARD, the optical metaphor is somewhat weaker" (Ishii and Ullmer, p. 240). Is that a flaw, or the annotation doing its job?
- **Zoom out:** judge a portfolio by CHI's criteria for a study, or by its own? Who annotates? Bridge to anchor 4.
- **Strong report:** cites p. 944 or p. 945; slides 11 to 13 (Rams test) or 15 (pushback); the reviewer names the author's best point; their own project as a portfolio.
- **Trap:** the reviewer rejecting on taste. Ask for the sentence that would let them say no.

## Card 3. Student v studio head

### Front

**Card 3: Student v studio head**
- **Question:** Who benefits when research through design has no written criteria?
- **Roles:** A, a first-year PhD student. B, a studio head.
- **Object:** one CHI pile: the student's first paper beside the studio head's ten-year portfolio.
- **Rule:** five minutes, then report in one sentence each: what you argued and the other side's best point.

### Back: Role A, student

- Gaver grants standards help "clarify expectations for research through design both within and outside the design community" (p. 937).
- Without written criteria, reviewers fall back on track record, which I lack (our reading).
- Definitions were never neutral: "largely as the pragmatic result of decisions about government funding of higher education" (Frayling 1993, p. 1). Better written (our reading).
- **Do not say:** "Gaver protects his studio." The text gives no motive; argue from effects.
- **If stuck:** which written criterion would help you most?

### Back: Role B, studio head

- Standards risk "self-policing" that is "overly restrictive" of work that can "challenge status quo thinking" (Gaver, pp. 937 to 938).
- Norms "are political acts to be approached with care" (Gaver, p. 945). Who writes them?
- "Disciplinary legitimacy" (p. 937) is the wrong goal: "consensus can come to look like constraint" (Gaver, p. 946).
- **Do not say:** "criteria only hurt." Gaver has "some sympathy with these concerns" (p. 937).
- **If stuck:** name a criterion that would reject work you value.

### Facilitator note (Oliver only)

- **Call:** "The student and the studio head: who benefits from no criteria?"
- **Zoom in:** the same page, p. 937, has both "disciplinary legitimacy" and "self-policing". Which phrase decides it for a first paper?
- **Zoom out:** does the same rule weigh the same on a studio head and a first-year student? Bridge to anchor 1.
- **Strong report:** cites p. 937 or p. 945; slide 5 (the call for standards) or slide 8; the student concedes that criteria can police; ends on their own first submission.
- **Trap:** a student taking the role as their own view, or giving Gaver a motive. Note that Frayling cuts both ways: the studio head can use it too.

## Card 4. Popper v Lakatos

### Front

**Card 4: Popper v Lakatos**
- **Question:** Is this good theory?
- **Roles:** A, Popper. B, Lakatos.
- **Object:** Tangible Bits: "one user interface metaphor that can be applied seamlessly across the metaDESK, ambientROOM, and transBOARD platforms" (p. 240).
- **Rule:** five minutes, then report in one sentence each: what you argued and the other side's best point.

### Back: Role A, Popper

- "irrefutability is not a virtue of a theory (as people often think) but a vice" (Popper, quoted in Gaver, p. 939). What would refute this?
- The authors: "On the transBOARD, the optical metaphor is somewhat weaker" (Ishii and Ullmer, p. 240). Refuted, or rescued?
- Gaver grants such theories are "not falsifiable in principle" (p. 940). A vice.
- **Do not say:** anything about physics. Argue about this sentence.
- **If stuck:** rewrite the claim so a study could prove it false.

### Back: Role B, Lakatos

- Good programmes "predict novel facts" (Lakatos, quoted in Gaver, p. 940). What did this lead to?
- The authors: "a great source of inspiration for future work" (Ishii and Ullmer, p. 240). Confirmations are "testimony to the fertility of the overarching theory" (Gaver, p. 941).
- One weak platform does not end a programme: "theories that are sometimes right" (Gaver, p. 941).
- **Do not say:** "it was fertile, so it is true." Fertility is not truth (our reading).
- **If stuck:** name one later design it helped produce.

### Facilitator note (Oliver only)

- **Call:** "Popper and Lakatos: is the optical metaphor good theory?"
- **Zoom in:** "seamlessly" against "somewhat weaker", both on p. 240. Did the paper refute itself?
- **Zoom out:** if fertility shows only over years, is one paper the right unit? Gaver: portfolios are to research programmes as artefacts are to theory (p. 944). Bridge to anchor 2.
- **Strong report:** cites p. 939, p. 940 or p. 941 and the Tangible Bits page; slide 9 (two tests) or slide 19 (course readings); Popper concedes the claim inspired work; applies the test to one claim from their own project.
- **Trap:** arguing about physics instead of the Tangible Bits claim. Read the sentence aloud and ask again.

## Card 5. Artist v scientist

### Front

**Card 5: Artist v scientist**
- **Question:** Does design make many worlds, so shared standards do not fit?
- **Roles:** A, an artist. B, a scientist.
- **Object:** the Prayer Companion (CHI 2010): news feeds shown to cloistered nuns as prayer prompts, ten months.
- **Rule:** five minutes, then report in one sentence each: what you argued and the other side's best point.

### Back: Role A, artist

- Design is "a generative discipline, able to create multiple new worlds rather than describing a single existing one" (Gaver, p. 943).
- Even assuming shared standards are needed: "potentially repressive acts of ontological politics" (p. 943). Keep "potentially".
- After the iPad, all are "designing for a different world, one in which the iPad exists" (p. 943).
- **Do not say:** "Gaver says design is not science." He hedges: "Personally, I tend to think" (p. 941).
- **If stuck:** what would a standard assume about the nuns?

### Back: Role B, scientist

- Of two incompatible accounts, "one must be better than the other" (Gaver, p. 943, on natural science). The convent is one real place.
- Without shared standards nothing accumulates: what does the next designer learn? (our reading)
- Gaver: "greater consensus may be emerging in research through design than is sometimes acknowledged" (p. 945).
- **Do not say:** "Gaver thinks anything goes." Many worlds is one of his three readings, hedged ("potentially", p. 943).
- **If stuck:** what should every reviewer agree on?

### Facilitator note (Oliver only)

- **Call:** "The artist and the scientist: one world or many?"
- **Zoom in:** the iPad sentence (Gaver, p. 943). Did the Prayer Companion make a new world in that sense, or study an old one?
- **Zoom out:** name the single world your own project assumes. Is "all three readings, for different parts of the field" an answer or a dodge? Bridge to anchor 3.
- **Strong report:** cites p. 943 with a hedge; slide 10 (three readings and the vote); the artist concedes that something must accumulate; one sentence on their own project's world.
- **Trap:** either player dropping "potentially" or turning "Personally, I tend to think" into a thesis.

## Facilitator summary (Oliver only, one page)

**Clock (from slide 22, ours):**
- 0 to 5: warm-up hands, "is your project research through design?"
- 5 to 10: pairs, one card each, five minutes. Hand out cards 1 to 5 in order; with more than five pairs, start again at card 1.
- 10 to 24: pairs report, then two or three anchors from slide 21. Pick anchors from what the pairs raised.
- 24 to 30: closing round, "One thing I now expect from research through design."

**Calling on a pair:** call the card, not the person. "The reviewer and the author: who won?" Then ask the losing side for the other side's best point. Two sentences from you, then a question back.

**Grading pattern (note who spoke; graded after class):**
- cited the paper with a page;
- cited the talk with a slide;
- acknowledged the other side's strongest point;
- applied the argument to their own project.

**If a pair finishes early:** give them the reserve question, "Does this essay meet its own standard? Is it an annotated portfolio with one item?" (Gaver, pp. 939 and 945). Ask them to report it last if time allows.

**Common traps across cards:**
- agreement in thirty seconds: ask what the loser would have to concede;
- a role taken as personal view: thank them, then ask what they think themselves;
- a motive given to Gaver or the critics: "the text does not say why; what does it say?";
- the critics paraphrased as "design should be a science": point to Zimmerman et al. 2007, p. 499.

**If silent:** wait, count five, then ask narrower with a page (slide 22, If asked).

**Oliver's own project stays out.**
