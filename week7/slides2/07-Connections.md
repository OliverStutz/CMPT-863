**Speaker notes**
- **Say:** Gaver cites none of our course readings, so each of these links is ours. First, Tangible Bits. Ishii and Ullmer join three platforms with one optical metaphor and write about what holds them together. We think that is the clearest annotated portfolio we have read, fifteen years before Gaver named the form. Second, Greenberg and Buxton. They make Gaver's Popper diagnosis for HCI evaluation: our studies look for confirmation. But their cure is split: existence proofs for the really new, refutation for the mainstream. Gaver drops refutation as the test for design theory. Third, the gesture study by Wobbrock, Morris and Wilson. By 2020 a review counted over two hundred studies that used that method, which dates from 2005. By Gaver's fertility test it is a thriving programme, and it is ordinary empirical science. So fertility rescues design theory without setting design apart. Fourth, Shin and colleagues in week six tested a design for four hours in a realistic setting. That achieves the realistic half of what Gaver called inconceivable. But nearly every participant guessed the purpose, so the unambiguous half is missing, and Gaver is half right. (about 175 words)
- **Transition:** Our verdict, in three sentences we would say to his face.
- **Watch out:**
  - Ishii and Ullmer: "one user interface metaphor" across three platforms (metaDESK, transBOARD, ambientROOM) is their p. 240. The "annotated portfolio before the name" reading is ours.
  - Greenberg and Buxton: "confirmatory evidence" is their p. 113; the split cure is p. 118. Gaver's matching diagnosis is p. 940. Gaver does not drop evaluation; he drops refutation as the test for design theory.
  - Wobbrock, Morris and Wilson 2009 extended a method begun in 2005; the 216-study count is from a 2020 review, cited in `../slides/09-Connections.md`. Say "over two hundred studies that used the method," never "follow-up studies" of the 2009 paper.
  - Shin et al.: "ecological validity" is their p. 5; the guessed purpose is p. 7. Gaver's "inconceivable" is p. 940. The reading "half right" is ours.
  - If anyone asks about Olsen or Wobbrock and Kientz, the alternates are below.
- **If asked:**
  - *Q: Why is Tangible Bits a portfolio and not just three systems?* A: Because the paper articulates what joins them (the optical metaphor, the move from GUI to tangible) and what separates them (desk, board, room), with the systems kept particular. That is Gaver's balance of example and annotation (p. 945). The authors did not call it that.
  - *Q: Doesn't Greenberg and Buxton's split cure fit Gaver well?* A: Partly. Existence proofs for the really new is close to Gaver's "ultimate particular." But Greenberg and Buxton keep refutation for mainstream work, where Gaver's unfalsifiability argument applies to all design theory. We argue their cure is the more careful one.
  - *Q: What is the counter in the Wobbrock case exactly?* A: Gaver uses fertility to rescue design theory from Popper. An empirical method is also fertile. So fertility is not a property that distinguishes design from science, which weakens any reading of the essay as "design is a different kind of thing."
  - *Q: What did Shin et al. do?* A: A slow-moving robotic monitor for posture correction, tested over four hours in a realistic office setting (Week 6). Realistic, yes, which is half of what Gaver called inconceivable. Unambiguous, no: actuation always came on day 2, and 11 of 12 participants guessed the purpose (their p. 7), which is the kind of contamination Gaver's argument predicts.
- **Alternates (from `../slides/09-Connections.md`):**
  - **Olsen 2007:** three diverse solutions support a claim about "the space between." Gaver's portfolio "establishes an area" (p. 944). We argue Olsen states what Gaver only sketches: more diverse examples make a stronger general claim.
  - **Wobbrock and Kientz 2016:** "wary" of constraint, yet "embrace giving definition" (p. 44). Gaver: "wary" of convergence (p. 937). The debate in one word.
