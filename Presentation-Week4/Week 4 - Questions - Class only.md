# 13 Questions: Did they read the paper?


**Q13.1** (quick check (sentence))
The paper lists its contributions as four numbered items. What are they, and what does the abstract present that the numbered list leaves out?


**Q13.2** (quick check (sentence))
The Discussion says "Figure 2 indicates that agreement is low after the first seven referents." Which figure actually shows this, which seven referents are meant, and what design advice does the paper draw from it?


**Q13.3** (quick check (sentence))
When asked to zoom in, one participant said "Oh, that's the same as enlarge." What does the paper conclude from simplifications like this, and how does the final set use them?


**Q13.4** (quick check (sentence))
Name the four dimensions of the paper's taxonomy, and explain in one sentence what the flow dimension distinguishes.


**Q13.5** (short answer (paragraph))
The paper describes using a computer as a dialogue and then deliberately turns the study into a monologue. Reconstruct its argument from dialogue and feedback, and name the second thing, besides Windows and Macintosh elements, that the prompts deliberately left out.


**Q13.6** (short answer (paragraph))
Participants rated each gesture for "good match" and for "easy to perform." For each rating, which predictors did the paper test and find had no significant effect, and what does that pattern of null results show?


**Q13.7** (short answer (paragraph))
Four referents got no dedicated gesture. Which, why, in two different ways, and what does the paper say this tells us about the set?


**Q13.8** (short answer (paragraph))
Under "A Land Beyond the Screen," what did participants do, which gestures in the final set depend on that space, and does the paper's reassurance about tables without edge sensing hold?


**Q13.9** (short answer (paragraph))
The paper makes two separate recommendations about on-screen widgets. What are they, what evidence is each based on, and which question from the method section do they answer?


**Q13.10** (analysis (pair discussion))
Figure 2 reports the form dimension separately for the right and the left hand. Why is it split, how do the two hands differ, and what does that do to the paper's "about 72% mouse-like" claim?


**Q13.11** (analysis (pair discussion))
The paper uses the figure "each author proposed only 43.5%" for two different arguments. What are they, and does the comparison support the claim the authors set out to test?


**Q13.12** (analysis (pair discussion))
On p. 1091 the paper recommends sensing above the table, and on the same page it admits what removing the dialogue cost. Put the two together: is "acting above the table" a fact about users or a product of the method, and what does the paper itself concede about how feedback would change it?

# 14 Questions: Did they listen to the presentation?


**Q14.1** (quick check (sentence))
The paper gives no date for its study. How did we pin down when it was done, and by when was it finished?


**Q14.2** (quick check (sentence))
What pixel density did we compute for the study's Surface prototype, what did we compare it with, and does the paper itself blame resolution for anything?


**Q14.3** (quick check (sentence))
Name one gesture in the final set that we called culture-bound, and one verified example of a mark or movement whose meaning changes across cultures.


**Q14.4** (quick check (sentence))
Which course reading did we use to argue that elicitation can pull toward the familiar, and what evidence from the paper did we pair it with?


**Q14.5** (short answer (paragraph))
Someone asks, "So was the study done to protect Microsoft from lawsuits?" What did we answer, and on what evidence?


**Q14.6** (short answer (paragraph))
The paper found that gestures rated easy took longer to perform than gestures rated difficult. How did we read the timing data, and how does our explanation differ from the paper's?


**Q14.7** (short answer (paragraph))
Each author matched only 43.5% of the users' set. We said this measures overlap, not quality, and we tied it to one of Olsen's steps for "empowering new design participants." Which step does it support, which claim does it leave open, and what did we say the 19.1% leaves undecided?


**Q14.8** (short answer (paragraph))
What can a sample of 20 people support, what can it not, and why did we say "norm is not the same as sufficient"?


**Q14.9** (pair discussion (analysis))
About 72% of gestures were mouse-like one-point touches or paths. We gave more than one source for that number. Name the sources, and explain why the study's data cannot tell them apart.


**Q14.10** (pair discussion (analysis))
Our one-sentence reading was "The lasting contribution is the method. The evidence in this paper for the method is thinner than its influence." Make the case for each half, and say what we conceded.
