#### Designing a Study, Method, or Data Analysis

Your abstract should address:

- **Research question:** What are you trying to learn, and what would your work contribute theoretically?
- **Hypothesis:** What do you expect to find, and why? Your hypothesis should be specific and testable within a 10-week project.
- **Related work:** Discuss 4–5 relevant published papers, how they inform your project, and how your work extends them.
- **Method:** Explain how you will test your hypothesis and why this approach is appropriate.
    - **Study design:** What exactly will you do?
    - **Measures:** What will you measure, and how?
    - **Validity:** Why does your study design adequately answer your research question and test your hypothesis?
- **Recruitment:** Who will participate, and how will you recruit them?

For APA formatting, see [APA Style](http://www.apastyle.org/learn/tutorials/basics-tutorial.aspx). For an example of the level of detail expected in the final paper, see _[Dynamic Speedometer: Dashboard Redesign to Discourage Drivers from Speeding](http://hci.stanford.edu/research/speedometer/LBR-197-kumar.pdf)_.

##### Iterate Early

Treat this abstract as a working research plan. We encourage you to revise it repeatedly and seek feedback through office hours or informal conversations with course staff. You may change direction after submitting the draft, but projects generally benefit from settling on a clear research question early.

##### Tips for Studies with Participants

- Provide important instructions in writing so they are clear and consistent across participants.
- Instrument your system to log relevant user actions.
- Pilot early. Study procedures, like interfaces, almost always require iteration.
- Give participants a realistic and motivating scenario when asking them to complete a task.
