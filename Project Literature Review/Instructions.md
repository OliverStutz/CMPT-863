Expand the related work section you had in your abstract. The literature review should situate your project within prior work. Include relevant **scholarly research** and, where appropriate, **industry articles, books, trade publications, and existing products**. As a general benchmark, you should identify at least **15 relevant sources**.

Organize the review into **thematic subsections**, similar to the Related Work sections of papers we read in class. Do not summarize sources one by one. Instead, synthesize groups of papers to explain:

- What approaches, technologies, or theories already exist
- What we currently know from prior work
- What remains unresolved or underexplored
- How your project differs from and extends existing research

By the end of the review, readers should understand that you have covered the relevant literature, identified a meaningful gap, and established why your project offers a novel contribution.

For reference, Related Work sections in CHI or UIST papers are often around **1,000 words**. At this stage, you may write somewhat more while developing your thinking, but keep the review under **1,500 words**. Follow the citation and reference format specified in the proposal guidelines.
