## The assignment (verbatim from `Instructions.md`)

Expand the related work section you had in your abstract. The literature review should situate your project within prior work. Include relevant scholarly research and, where appropriate, industry articles, books, trade publications, and existing products. As a general benchmark, you should identify at least 15 relevant sources. Organize the review into thematic subsections, similar to the Related Work sections of papers we read in class. Do not summarize sources one by one. Instead, synthesize groups of papers to explain: what approaches, technologies, or theories already exist; what we currently know from prior work; what remains unresolved or underexplored; how your project differs from and extends existing research. By the end of the review, readers should understand that you have covered the relevant literature, identified a meaningful gap, and established why your project offers a novel contribution. Related Work sections in CHI or UIST papers are often around 1,000 words; keep the review under 1,500 words. Follow the citation and reference format specified in the proposal guidelines (APA).

Rubric: Quality, 4 points (Excellent 4, Good 3, Adequate 2, Poor 1). Style, 2 points (Good 2, Poor 1).

## What to do

1. **Mark it.** Give the section a Quality mark out of 4 and a Style mark out of 2 as a strict but fair HCI instructor would, with one paragraph of reasons each. Say exactly what separates it from Excellent.
2. **The four questions.** For each of the four (what exists; what we know; what is unresolved; how the project differs and extends), quote the sentences that answer it and judge whether a marker skimming the section would see the answer. Say where the answer is weak, implicit or missing.
3. **Synthesis versus summary.** Does the section synthesise across sources, or list them one after another? Point to any sentence that reads as a one-paper summary.
4. **Accuracy.** Spot-check at least eight factual statements against the full texts in `references/` (pdftotext -layout; .txt for Madanay and Sharma) or `search/verified-view.md`: the SEC p. 85 reasoning; Lowry (mostly symbolic; the right-questions belief; who judged that non-experts cannot interpret answers; the 40% survey median; expertise rather than power, p. 799; the two theories, p. 784); Opdenbusch (silence from fear of exposure, budget as the only decision, CISOs decide what reaches the board); Aamir (external assurance and accepting management's account); Schilke; the Chen 2026 and D. Kim 2026 characterisation (expert consulted the AI, or it ran on its own); Danry and Lu (belief in claims or trust in the AI). Verdict per statement: correct, imprecise (exact fix), wrong (exact fix). Also judge whether the hedged inferences in paragraph 3 are acceptable in a related-work section or overreach.
5. **Style and register.** Clarity, precision, sentence length, signposting, consistency of tense and voice, plain English without jargon, no padding. The author wants precise, direct writing with good words but not an overly sophisticated register, and no em-dashes. Flag anything that reads as awkward or unnatural, with a replacement.
6. **Length and proportion.** The section is about 720 words of a 1,500-word review with five sections. Say what you would cut to bring it to about 450 to 500 words without losing an answer to any of the four questions, with exact sentences.
7. **APA.** Check the in-text citation forms in the section and the seven entries in its reference list (the four forward-cited examples have entries in v6's list).
